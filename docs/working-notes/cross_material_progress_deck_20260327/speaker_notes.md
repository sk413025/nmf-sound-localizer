# Speaker Notes - Cross-Material Progress Deck

## Slide 1 - Cross-Material Progress

Open by stating that this is a progress snapshot rather than a final manuscript-defense talk. The scope is deliberately narrow: five-material cross-material white-noise progress from March 24-25, 2026. The audience should leave with one sentence in mind: the physical encoder reproduces across objects, and cardboard box is the current primary screening target.

## Slide 2 - Question and Setup

Frame the talk around three questions. First, does the encoder reproduce across materials at all? Second, does the low-rank structure survive when we move from one object to five? Third, does any of that actually matter for downstream screening? Emphasize that this deck excludes speech on purpose so the message stays clean.

## Slide 3 - Cross-Material H Reproduces

Use this slide as breadth evidence, not as a ranking slide. The main message is that all five materials retain structured angle-frequency patterns under the same normalization view. Mention that coherence is part of the physics inspection layer, but stop short of implying that it decides the downstream winner.

## Slide 4 - Low-Rank Structure Persists Across Materials

This slide connects the new cross-material progress back to the existing manuscript logic. The important point is not the exact curve shape; it is that `rank95` remains small across the object set. Say explicitly that low-rank structure appears to be a reusable physical property rather than a single-object accident.

## Slide 5 - Screening Outcome: B Primary, W Backup

This is the decision slide. Lead with the result first: B is primary, W is backup. Then explain the negative-control insight: P has the strongest coherence but does not win downstream. That contrast is useful because it shows why the screening story cannot be reduced to a single physics scalar.

## Slide 6 - Why Screening Is Not Just Energy

Slow down slightly here. The contribution is not "we found the loudest object." The contribution is that frequency-localized directional structure appears to govern screening performance better than aggregate response energy. This is the physical interpretation slide that turns the empirical ranking into a mechanism-oriented story.

## Slide 7 - Status, Limits, and Next Steps

Be explicit about limits. The bundle inventory is correct and the processing chain is executable, but strict raw-rerecord compliance is still not fully proven from workspace evidence alone. End on the forward path: keep B first, keep W as backup, and broaden validation before making any bigger cross-material claim.

## Backup - Full Screening Metrics

Use this only if someone asks for the numbers behind the rank order. The clean answer is that B wins on overall downstream balance, W wins on fallback value, and the rest remain informative but not selected.

## Backup - Matched Greedy vs Hybrid Benchmark

Use this only if someone asks whether the current cross-material story is really about the guided solver. The answer is no. This benchmark is helpful as a comparator path and as negative-control evidence, but it is not the center of the current progress update.

## Backup - Provenance Chain

Use this if the audience wants to know whether the work is executable and traceable. The main point is that the deck is grounded in committed audit, ingest, H-reproduction, screening, and support bundles rather than in ad hoc screenshots.
