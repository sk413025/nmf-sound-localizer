Results: exp_m32_k16_full_gain100_ep50 — Gain=100 soft-metric fix + leakage audit

Experiment context (REQUIRED):
- Background: Prior reports showed “soft capture” collapsing in Mids/Highs due to an epsilon floor (denominator uses +1e-6) when signal energies are ~1e-8.
- Motivation: Verify that applying Gain=100 consistently (generation + evaluation) removes this numeric artifact and yields sensible band-wise ceilings.
- Purpose: Rerun the M=65 (lags −32..32), K=16, Tw=16 DT-vs-OMP pipeline on speech260 (angle=90°), then perform a structured leakage/bug audit because results looked “too good to be true”.
- Expected: Soft capture should no longer be pinned near 0% at high frequencies; at K=16, DT should approach OMP’s oracle ceiling (but not necessarily generalize).

Key findings:
- Gain=100 eliminates the epsilon-floor artifact; soft capture is no longer artificially near-zero in Mids/Highs.
- DT can exceed OMP at small K (efficiency > 100%) because DT is not constrained to follow OMP’s greedy path.
- “Perfect” capture at K=16 is not necessarily suspicious: with Tw=16 and K=16, per-frequency LS can behave like an interpolator over a short window if dictionary atoms are sufficiently independent.

Leakage / bug audit (high-level):
- No trivial duplication: 0 exact duplicate blocks under signature sha1(actions||scores).
- Trajectory blocks do not include clip identifiers; therefore rigorous clip-level leakage quantification is not possible for this run.
- Training likely has train/val contamination because the dataset is flattened into one sample per frequency per time-window block and `random_split` operates at this flat-sample level.
- Evaluation is in-distribution (same dataset roots as generation). RTG is computed from residual energy over the observed LDV window Y; this is fine for offline/windowed inference but is a lookahead feature if deployment requires streaming without access to the full Tw-block.
