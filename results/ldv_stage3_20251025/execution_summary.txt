First principles: USM training learns spectral dictionary W via NMF factorization
V ≈ WH where V is the input magnitude spectrogram

Mathematical relationships:
- Original dataset (mean=6.82e-03): Higher magnitude → more atoms with larger values
- Box dataset (mean=8.31e-04): Lower magnitude → sparser atom distributions
- IrregularBox dataset (mean similar to Box): Comparable sparsity patterns

Signal processing theory:
- IS divergence (β=0) appropriate for magnitude spectra (scale-invariant)
- 50 atoms/speaker balances expressiveness vs overfitting for white noise
- Geometric mean pooling in TF estimation preserves multiplicative scale

Information theory:
- Higher W.max in Box/IrregularBox (>1.0) vs Original (0.172) indicates
  different amplitude normalization regimes captured in dictionary atoms
- Sparsity (~40-45% non-zero) provides compact spectral basis representation
